Agentic vs generative AI

When AI stops answering and starts acting.

A decision briefing for leaders on the practical difference between generative and agentic AI — and why that distinction changes budgets, governance, and risk far more than it changes tooling.

The problem this solves

The terms are used interchangeably by vendors, which hides the part that matters: generative AI produces output a human then uses, while agentic AI takes actions with consequences. The governance and cost implications are not comparable.

What the engagement should produce

  • A clear working distinction leadership can apply to any vendor pitch
  • The cost difference: per-request output versus ongoing autonomous operation
  • The governance difference: reviewing output versus constraining actions
  • A map of which of your workflows are genuinely agentic candidates
  • A sequencing view: what to prove with generative AI before funding agents

Ways to work together

  • Leadership decision briefing
  • Board and executive session
  • Vendor evaluation support
  • Portfolio mapping workshop

Who it is for

Executives, technology leaders, and investment committees evaluating agentic AI proposals or vendor claims.

No black-box transformation. The goal is internal capability, explicit ownership, and evidence that the workflow improved the work.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Is agentic AI just a marketing term?

The marketing use is loose, but the underlying distinction is real and consequential: whether the system takes actions with side effects. That determines your governance and cost model.

Should we skip generative and go straight to agents?

Rarely. Generative workflows prove the value and expose the data and process gaps cheaply. Agents built on unproven processes fail expensively.
