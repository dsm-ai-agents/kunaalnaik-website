Measurement

Prove the training changed the work.

A measurement approach for L&D and transformation teams who must justify AI spend with something more defensible than satisfaction scores.

The problem this solves

AI training is usually measured by attendance and happy sheets, which say nothing about whether the work improved. Meanwhile the baseline needed to prove improvement is never captured, so the question becomes unanswerable after the fact.

What the engagement should produce

  • A baseline captured before delivery — without it, no improvement can be shown
  • Adoption measures: who is still using the workflow at 30, 60, and 90 days
  • Time measures on the specific workflow trained, not self-reported global estimates
  • Quality and rework measures, since faster output that needs more correction is a loss
  • Review burden — the verification cost AI adds, counted honestly
  • Risk measures: policy breaches, escalations, and near misses

Ways to work together

  • Measurement design session before a programme begins
  • Baseline capture support
  • 90-day review and reporting
  • Board and sponsor reporting pack

Who it is for

L&D, transformation, and finance sponsors accountable for demonstrating a return on AI training spend.

No black-box transformation. The goal is internal capability, explicit ownership, and evidence that the workflow improved the work.

How scope is decided

Send the audience, workflow or business priority, current tools, constraints, and desired outcome. The first response will identify the smallest useful starting point and any missing information—not force a standard package.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Can you guarantee a productivity percentage?

No, and any provider quoting one before seeing your processes is guessing. Outcomes depend on your workflows, data, and follow-through.

When should measurement be designed?

Before delivery. Retrofitting measurement is the most common reason AI training cannot be justified afterwards.

Why count review burden as a cost?

Because it is one. AI shifts effort from producing to verifying, and a measurement model that ignores that overstates the gain.
