RFP template

An RFP that surfaces real capability.

A structure L&D and procurement teams can use to compare AI training providers on the same basis — free to use, no email required, including questions Kunaal could fail.

The problem this solves

AI training RFPs typically ask for tool coverage and day rates, which every provider can answer identically. The questions that separate capable providers from confident ones are usually missing.

What the engagement should produce

  • Audience and population definition, broken down by function and seniority
  • Required outcomes stated as changes in work, not topics covered
  • Governance requirements: data handling, approvals, and boundaries
  • Proof requirements: named, verifiable evidence rather than participant totals
  • Follow-through obligations for the 90 days after delivery
  • Evaluation criteria and scoring weights agreed before responses arrive

Ways to work together

  • Section-by-section RFP structure
  • Question bank that exposes teaching-only providers
  • Scoring rubric for comparing responses
  • Reference-check question set

Who it is for

L&D, HR, and procurement teams running a competitive process for AI training or enablement.

No black-box transformation. The goal is internal capability, explicit ownership, and evidence that the workflow improved the work.

How scope is decided

Send the audience, workflow or business priority, current tools, constraints, and desired outcome. The first response will identify the smallest useful starting point and any missing information—not force a standard package.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Is the template gated?

No. It is on this page, free to copy, with no form. Gating a buyer's tool to harvest emails is exactly the behaviour that makes procurement distrust vendors.

Can we use it to evaluate other providers?

That is the intent. It is written to be provider-neutral, and Kunaal can be scored against it alongside everyone else.
