Selection criteria

How to choose a trainer you would rehire.

A buyer's guide written by a practitioner, including the criteria that predict adoption and the signals that predict an expensive, forgettable session.

The problem this solves

The market is crowded with trainers whose main evidence is participant counts and self-awarded superlatives. Neither predicts whether anything changes in the work after the room empties.

What the engagement should produce

  • Implementation evidence — has the trainer shipped workflows, or only taught tools?
  • Role-based design — is the curriculum built per function, or one deck for everyone?
  • Governance depth — can they discuss permissions, approvals, and data boundaries credibly?
  • Verifiable proof — named, checkable outcomes rather than large round numbers
  • Follow-through — what happens in the 90 days after delivery
  • Refusal discipline — a trainer who says what AI should not do is more trustworthy

Ways to work together

  • Selection criteria you can put straight into an RFP
  • Reference questions that reveal real capability
  • Red flags: self-ranking lists, unverifiable totals, guaranteed outcomes, tool-inventory curricula
  • A scoring approach for comparing proposals on the same basis

Who it is for

L&D, HR, procurement, and transformation leaders evaluating trainers or writing an RFP.

No black-box transformation. The goal is internal capability, explicit ownership, and evidence that the workflow improved the work.

How scope is decided

Send the audience, workflow or business priority, current tools, constraints, and desired outcome. The first response will identify the smallest useful starting point and any missing information—not force a standard package.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Is it a conflict of interest for a trainer to publish this?

It is a stated bias, which is why the criteria are written to be checkable against any provider, including Kunaal. Use them to disqualify him if he does not meet them.

What is the single strongest signal?

Whether the trainer has implemented the workflows they teach. Teaching-only providers cannot answer the operational questions that decide adoption.

What about certifications?

They indicate exposure, not capability. Ask what the trainer has built and what broke.
