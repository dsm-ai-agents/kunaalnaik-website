AI training for engineering

AI for engineering beyond autocomplete.

Training for engineering managers and technical teams already using AI coding tools, focused on the surrounding work — planning, documentation, review, reporting — and on agent workflows with real guardrails.

The problem this solves

Engineering teams adopt AI coding assistants individually and stall there. Nobody owns the review standard, generated code enters the codebase unexamined, and the non-coding work that consumes manager time stays manual.

What the engagement should produce

  • A documentation workflow that keeps architecture notes current instead of stale
  • A planning and status-reporting loop that does not consume a manager's week
  • A review standard for AI-generated code, including what must be read line by line
  • An agent workflow for a bounded repetitive task, with permissions and a blast-radius limit
  • Measures for review throughput, documentation coverage, and rework rate

Ways to work together

  • Engineering leadership briefing
  • Two-day technical workflow workshop
  • Agent workflow implementation lab
  • Team standards and review-practice sprint

Who it is for

VPs of engineering, engineering managers, tech leads, and platform teams who want team-level practice rather than individual tool adoption.

No black-box transformation. The goal is internal capability, explicit ownership, and evidence that the workflow improved the work.

How scope is decided

Send the audience, workflow or business priority, current tools, constraints, and desired outcome. The first response will identify the smallest useful starting point and any missing information—not force a standard package.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Our developers already use AI coding tools. What does this add?

Individual tool use is not team capability. This addresses the shared standards — review, documentation, permissions, blast radius — plus the planning and reporting work that tools alone do not touch.

Is this framework or language specific?

No. The workflows and standards apply across stacks. Exercises use your repositories and your review process where access permits.
